- Some things to know (and memorise)
	- Standard deck of cards: 4 suites (S,C,H,D), 13 cards in each suite (A,2,...,9,10,J,Q,K), i.e a total of 52 cards. Hearts and Diamonds are red, Spades and Clubs are black.
1. At an Ice cream shop I plan to get two scoops and a topping. There are three scoop flavours: Chocolate, Vanilla, Strawberry and 5 possible toppings: granola, frozen mangoes, dubai chocolate, chocolate syrup, and M&Ms. Find the number of different icecream combinations (with toppings) I can get
2. Draw a four cards out of a standard deck with replacement
	1. How many possible sequences exist for the draws in total? (count 3s, 5h, Jd, Kh as one possible draw sequences)
	2. Now draw the four cards without replacement. How many possible sequences exist?
	3. Still drawing the cards without replacement, how many different sets of 4 cards can you form?
	4. (From here on, draw the cards without replacement) Whats the probability of not drawing any hearts?
	5. Whats the probability of drawing atleast one clubs?
	6. Suppose you draw 12 cards now. What is the probability of drawing only 4 red cards? 
		- This is an important type of question to understand
		- The number of marked cards (red) draw from a population without replacement has something called a "Hypergeometric distribution" 
3. Consider a room with 10 people. Assume each person's birthday is equally distributed in a standard 365 days calendar year. 
	1. I choose two people from the room. What is the probability they share a birthday?
	2. How many possible pairs can you form from the people in this room?
	3. Each possible pair would have the same probability of sharing a birthday found in (1). What is the probability that atleast one pair of people share birthdays?
	4. Suppose now there are $n$ people in the room. Find the probability of atleast one pair sharing a common birthday within this room. 
	5. Find the smallest value of $n$ required for this probability to be above 50%, i.e the number of people in a room you'd need to expect atleast a 50% chance of sharing birthdays
